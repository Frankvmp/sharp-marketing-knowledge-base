# Product innovation and adoption

## Core statement

New products range from discontinuous innovations that create a new attribute set or category, through dynamic continuous innovations, to continuous innovations that make smaller changes. Adoption depends on perceived advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, and observability.[^innovation][^adoption]

## Development procedure

1. Classify the proposed change by how much customer behaviour it requires to change.
2. Test the offer's relative advantage against the current solution.
3. Assess compatibility with current practices and learning requirements.
4. Reduce complexity and make trial and benefits observable where possible.
5. Screen ideas, develop the product, test the market, and introduce only where evidence supports the offer.[^innovation][^adoption][^screening]

## Conditions and limits

Major discontinuous innovations can take time because customers must change behaviour. Novelty alone does not create adoption; a new offer can fail when customers cannot understand, try, or see its advantage.[^innovation][^adoption]

## Decision implications

Design product development around customer adoption barriers, not only technical feasibility. Treat market research and test marketing as evidence stages that can reject an appealing internal idea.

# Services, experience, and adaptation

## Core statement

Services are central to many offers, and customers may seek an experience rather than a physical good alone. Service and experience quality depend on the total delivery system, including people, access, credibility, and supporting goods.[^services][^experience]

## Design implications

Service firms can be transaction-focused or relationship-focused, and service offerings can be combined with physical goods. People often embody the service experience, while digital alternatives can add access or broaden the market without necessarily replacing the established delivery mode.[^services][^experience]

## Service-quality dimensions

Service quality is assessed through reliability, assurance, tangibles, empathy, responsiveness, and recovery. Their relative importance depends on the goods or services offered and the customer context.[^service-quality]

- **Reliability:** performing the promised service accurately.
- **Assurance:** competence, courtesy, credibility, and security.
- **Tangibles:** the physical appearance of staff, buildings, and equipment.
- **Empathy:** communication and understanding of the customer.
- **Responsiveness:** timely service.
- **Recovery:** rectifying service or product failures that cause customer dissatisfaction.[^service-quality]

## Conditions and limits

Trust and credibility matter where customers cannot judge quality in advance, but this condition is not unique to services; B2B buyers can also purchase complex goods on trust. Technology can substitute for, complement, or extend a service depending on the purpose customers seek.[^services][^experience]

## Decision implications

Define the experience customers seek and the delivery elements that make it credible and accessible. Adapt the offer as buyer needs, customer type, and available delivery technology change.

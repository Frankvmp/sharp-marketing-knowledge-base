# Product category life cycle and growth options

## Core statement

Product categories evolve as customer circumstances, technology, regulation, and competition change. The product category life cycle is a planning framework for interpreting introduction, growth, maturity, and decline, not a guarantee that every offer follows the same path.[^plc]

## Lifecycle reasoning

Use the category's sales and market conditions to assess the appropriate emphasis on product, price, distribution, advertising, and sales promotion. Growth conditions can favour investment and expansion; mature markets can require stronger competition, product development, market development, or product modification.[^plc][^maturity]

## Conditions and limits

A category may revive, extend, or change rather than move smoothly into irreversible decline. Minor modifications can attract attention or serve a need but do not automatically create additional category demand. The framework must be interpreted with current evidence about buyers, competitors, and the wider environment.[^plc][^maturity]

## Decision implications

Use lifecycle stage as a hypothesis about market conditions, then verify it with category evidence. Select growth options according to unmet demand and feasible capability rather than applying a generic stage recipe.

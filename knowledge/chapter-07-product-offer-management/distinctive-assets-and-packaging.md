# Distinctive assets and packaging

## Core statement

Distinctive assets help buyers recognise an offer and attribute communications to the correct brand. Names, logos, colours, shapes, characters, and packaging can make the product easier to find and marketing more efficient when they are consistently used and learned.[^assets][^packaging]

## Asset logic

Brand naming may use individual, company, combined, or range names. Distinctive assets need not have inherent meaning; their value arises from learned, reliable association with the offer. Packaging can carry identification, information, regulatory labelling, and product-protection functions.[^names][^assets][^packaging]

## Conditions and limits

Changing packaging or other distinctive assets can disrupt buyers' existing memory structures and recognition. A distinctive element requires repeated, consistent exposure to become a useful cue. Regulatory requirements can limit packaging and labelling choices.[^assets][^packaging]

## Decision implications

Before altering a recognisable cue, assess its role in purchase identification and correct advertising attribution. Preserve or deliberately transfer distinctive assets when changing an offer or its appearance.

# Total offer and core need

## Core statement

Customers respond to a total offer, not an isolated physical item. A product can include goods, services, information, experiences, and supporting marketing elements that together help customers fulfil an underlying need.[^product][^core]

## Reasoning procedure

1. Define the customer purpose or need to be fulfilled.
2. Identify the core product or service that directly enables that purpose.
3. Identify the additional elements that add convenience, attractiveness, confidence, or experience.
4. Check that product, price, availability, communication, and brand cues form a coherent offer.
5. Compare the added value of each element with its cost and the preferences of potential buyers.[^product][^core]

## Conditions and limits

Some buyers prefer an unbundled core offer at a lower price, while others value a complete package. A physical product may be acquired to enable a service the customer provides for themselves, and a service business may use the same physical goods in a different business model.[^product][^core]

## Decision implications

Start product decisions with the job or outcome customers seek, then test which elements genuinely add value. Do not infer the core need from manufacturing capability alone.

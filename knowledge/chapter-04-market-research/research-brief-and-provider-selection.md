# Research brief and provider selection

## Core statement

A research brief converts a managerial problem into a defined information requirement. It keeps research objectives distinct from marketing objectives, allowing the commissioned work to inform a decision rather than claim it can create a commercial outcome.[^brief]

## Definitions

- **Marketing objective:** the organisational outcome sought, such as increased sales or awareness.
- **Research objective:** the information required to support a decision about that outcome.
- **Research brief:** the commissioning document that states why research is needed, what it should establish, and the project constraints.
- **Research proposal:** the provider's plan for meeting the brief, including approach, cost, deliverables, and timing.[^brief][^proposal]

## Commissioning procedure

1. State the business background and the management problem.
2. Define one overarching research objective and the smaller questions needed to meet it.
3. Specify how the results will be used, the required deliverables, the available budget, timing, relevant prior material, and the client team.
4. Let the provider recommend the collection method unless there is a sound constraint or successful prior approach.
5. Clarify the problem and information need with the provider before accepting a proposal.[^brief]

## Provider selection

Assess a provider's understanding of the organisation and research problem, willingness to make useful suggestions, trust and working relationship, ability to meet timing, staff experience and qualifications, accreditation and reputation, and communication skill. Price alone is a weak selection rule: similar costs can mean a lower price reflects reduced service.[^selection]

## Conditions and limits

Research supplies information; it does not itself increase awareness, sales, or market share. A poorly defined brief can constrain the provider from proposing a better-fit method. Accreditation is a protection for the buyer, not proof that a particular design is suitable.[^brief]

## Decision implications

Before committing budget, require an explicit chain from business problem to information need, then from the proposed evidence to the decision it will support. Treat a method recommendation as provisional until its fit to the objective, population, and constraints is demonstrated.

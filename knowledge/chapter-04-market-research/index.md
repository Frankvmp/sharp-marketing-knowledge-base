# Chapter 4: Market research

This chapter turns a marketing decision problem into a research design, evidence base, analysis, and report. Use the linked concepts to determine what information is required, whether the evidence can support the intended inference, and how to communicate it without overstating certainty.

## Concept map

- [Research brief and provider selection](./research-brief-and-provider-selection.md) — define the information need and commission suitable research.
- [Secondary data and contextual knowledge](./secondary-data-and-contextual-knowledge.md) — use existing evidence and established patterns before new collection.
- [Qualitative research methods](./qualitative-research-methods.md) — identify issues, behaviours, and language without estimating prevalence.
- [Quantitative research methods](./quantitative-research-methods.md) — select structured collection methods by coverage, response, cost, and fit.
- [Sampling and representativeness](./sampling-and-representativeness.md) — determine whether sample results can be generalised.
- [Research design and B2B research](./research-design-and-b2b-research.md) — match timing and purpose to the decision and organisational context.
- [Data analysis and statistical interpretation](./data-analysis-and-statistical-interpretation.md) — identify patterns and limit inference to what the data supports.

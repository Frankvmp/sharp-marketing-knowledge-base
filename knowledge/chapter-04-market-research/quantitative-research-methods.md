# Quantitative research methods

## Core statement

Quantitative research uses structured collection to estimate the incidence of behaviours and issues. Method choice must fit the target population, contact route, need for interviewer support, required speed, budget, and risks to coverage and response.[^quantitative]

## Definitions

- **Quantitative data:** numerical counts, percentages, amounts, timings, or averages.
- **Syndicated data:** data collected by a research provider and sold to multiple clients.
- **Online panel:** a pre-recruited pool of people who agree to participate in research.
- **Response rate:** the proportion of selected people who participate.[^quantitative][^methods]

## Method comparison

Telephone research can support random geographic sampling and trained interviewer administration but faces declining fixed-line coverage and low cold-call response. Online surveys are fast, flexible, and low-cost at scale, yet internet access and non-random panel recruitment can limit generalisation. Mail surveys can reach some low-internet populations but are slow and vulnerable to low response and self-selection. In-person surveys can reach people immediately after a behaviour and support demonstrations, but are costly and location-constrained.[^methods]

## Evidence and data

Online panels can deliver large samples, selectable characteristics, quotas, speed, and fixed project costs. Their principal risks are incomplete population coverage, non-random recruitment, and difficulty verifying participant characteristics. Longitudinal panels offer repeated behavioural measurement when sample quality and response are actively maintained.[^syndicated][^panels]

## Conditions and limits

A larger or faster sample is not automatically representative. Social-media monitoring captures an unusual, self-selecting minority and may reflect heavy users or highly motivated commenters rather than the wider population. Biometric and neuromarketing claims require particular caution when evidence has not been independently replicated.[^social][^biometrics]

## Decision implications

Choose the method only after defining the population and the decision's required accuracy. Record coverage, recruitment, response, and verification limitations alongside the result; do not treat a convenient data stream as an unbiased market measure.

# Research design and B2B research

## Core statement

Research design specifies whether evidence is collected once or repeatedly and whether the purpose is exploratory, descriptive, or causal. Business-to-business research often requires additional design choices because populations are smaller and organisational decisions are distributed across people and roles.[^design][^b2b]

## Definitions

- **Cross-sectional research:** collection at one point in time to describe a market or customer group.
- **Longitudinal research:** repeated collection over time to detect change.
- **Exploratory research:** investigation of an issue or situation where little is known.
- **Descriptive research:** a snapshot that estimates what happens, to whom, when, and how often.
- **Causal research:** research intended to establish the relationship and direction between variables.[^design]

## Design procedure

1. Specify whether the decision requires a current snapshot, a trend, or an effect estimate.
2. Classify the purpose as exploratory, descriptive, or causal.
3. Choose collection frequency and whether the same people must be tracked.
4. Define every relevant organisational role when researching a business customer.
5. Review internal records and past research before new collection.[^design][^b2b]

## Evidence and data

Cross-sectional studies compare groups at a point in time. Longitudinal tracking can use different respondents drawn from the same population, while a panel follows the same people and links changes to events or interventions. Organisational research can sometimes census all customers, but it may need multiple respondents within each business to capture decision, use, and service experience.[^design][^b2b]

## Conditions and limits

Repeated measurement supports trend detection but does not itself establish causality. Same-person panels can be expensive to maintain because people move or stop responding. In B2B research, contact times, travel, and incentives must fit organisational practice; personal incentives may be inappropriate.[^design][^b2b]

## Decision implications

Select a research design according to the decision's time horizon and the type of inference needed. Do not call a one-time descriptive study an effect test, and do not treat a single organisational respondent as the whole customer's experience without checking role coverage.

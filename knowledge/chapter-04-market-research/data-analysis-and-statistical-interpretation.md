# Data analysis and statistical interpretation

## Core statement

Analysis should organise and summarise data so that decision-relevant patterns become visible. The permissible analysis is constrained by the data type, research design, sample selection, sample size, and question structure.[^analysis]

## Analysis procedure

1. Identify the analyses and decisions required before data collection.
2. Begin quantitative analysis with frequencies and simple descriptive summaries.
3. Examine the mean, median, mode, and range to understand distribution shape.
4. Use multivariate methods only when their assumptions and purpose are explicit.
5. Interpret significance with effect size, sample design, and non-sampling errors in view.
6. Seek repeated patterns across multiple datasets and conditions when generalisation matters.[^analysis][^significance][^msod]

## Definitions

- **Univariate analysis:** examination of one variable at a time.
- **Multivariate analysis:** examination of multiple variables and their statistical relationships.
- **Statistical significance:** an indication that a result is unlikely to arise from random sampling variation.
- **Effect size:** the magnitude of a difference or relationship.
- **Significant sameness:** a recurring pattern observed across multiple datasets and conditions.[^analysis][^significance][^msod]

## Conditions and limits

Unstructured qualitative data supports themes and verbatim reporting, not statistical testing. Statistical significance does not mean a result is important, causal, predictable, or free from non-response and response error. With a large sample, even small differences can be statistically significant. Complex or technologically sophisticated models are not automatically reliable, scientific, or useful.[^analysis][^significance][^msod]

## Reporting and presentation

A report should present the decision-relevant conclusion first, then background, objectives, method, findings, and recommendations. Present data using whole numbers where sampling error makes decimals misleading; sort tables by meaningful values, show totals where appropriate, use averages or medians to highlight patterns, and state the takeaway from each table or chart.[^reporting]

## Decision implications

Use simple summaries as the baseline and require explanation of assumptions behind complex methods. Treat one-off significant findings as hypotheses unless the pattern is replicated or the design supplies stronger causal evidence.

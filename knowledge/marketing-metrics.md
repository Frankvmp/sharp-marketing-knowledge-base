# Marketing metrics

## Core statement

Marketing metrics are measures used to evaluate marketing actions and brand performance, and to inform future marketing decisions. Sales and market share are important measures, but both require interpretation in context.[^sharp-2017-ch1-metrics]

## Reasoning rule

An agent should not treat short-term sales, market share, or profit as sufficient evidence that an action improved the brand's long-term position. The chapter warns that sales or profit can be increased today by reducing expenditure that supports later revenue, or by activity that trades future sales for immediate results.[^sharp-2017-ch1-metrics]

## Practical evaluation sequence

1. Define the action being evaluated and the outcome it is meant to affect.
2. Select current-performance measures, such as sales or market share.
3. Interpret those measures in market context rather than as self-explanatory.
4. Include measures relevant to future sales and risk.
5. Check the action's likely effect on [market-based assets](./market-based-assets.md).[^sharp-2017-ch1-metrics]

## Evidence and data

The chapter lists sales, profit contribution per sale, market share, discounted-sales percentage, repeat sales, churn, customer satisfaction, customer count, brand awareness, purchase frequency, advertising spend, share of voice, advertising exposure, distribution outlets, and sales per outlet as commonly used measures.[^sharp-2017-ch1-metrics]

## Scope and limits

This chapter supplies a framing principle and examples of metrics. It does not provide a complete causal evaluation method, a prescribed scorecard, or target values for a specific organisation.[^sharp-2017-ch1-metrics]

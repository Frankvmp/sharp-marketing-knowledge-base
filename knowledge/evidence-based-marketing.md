# Evidence-based marketing

## Core statement

Marketing science studies buying and selling and seeks generalised knowledge and recurring patterns. Evidence-based marketing combines two inputs: reliable generalised knowledge about how buyers and marketing interventions work, and situation-specific evidence and factual data used to support a decision.[^sharp-2017-ch1-evidence]

## Reasoning model

| Input | Function | Misuse to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generalised knowledge | Supplies tested patterns about buying and marketing | Treating a broad pattern as a complete answer for one specific situation |
| Situation-specific evidence | Grounds the decision in the current market, buyer, brand, and conditions | Treating one local observation as a universal law |
| Decision judgement | Connects both evidence types to an action | Replacing evidence with fashion, rumour, or unsupported intuition |

## Application sequence

1. State the marketing decision.
2. Identify relevant generalised knowledge from marketing science.
3. Gather the situation-specific factual evidence needed for this decision.
4. Separate evidence from assumptions and unknowns.
5. Make a recommendation that states how both inputs shaped it.

## Evidence and data

Marketing science is described as a study of the empirical world that develops generalised knowledge and recurring patterns. The chapter contrasts systematic research with knowledge based on theory, opinion, fashion, rumours, or guesses.[^sharp-2017-ch1-evidence]

## Scope and limits

This is a decision approach, not a claim that all marketing outcomes are predictable or that a current source settles every question. The agent should preserve uncertainty where the required situation-specific evidence is absent.[^sharp-2017-ch1-evidence]

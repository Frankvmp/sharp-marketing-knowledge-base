# Evidence-based marketing

## Core statement

Evidence-based marketing uses reliable knowledge about recurring market patterns together with current, situation-specific evidence to make better decisions. It treats intuition and prevailing theory as claims that require testing rather than as sufficient evidence.[^evidence]

## Mechanism and relationships

Marketing science develops generalisable knowledge about how buyers, advertising, prices, and markets work. Market research contributes current evidence about a particular context. Combining both reduces the risk of acting on untested beliefs or isolated anecdotes.[^evidence]

## Evidence and data

The chapter compares marketing decision-making with professional fields that rely on accumulated tested knowledge, and uses historical examples to show how entrenched theories can persist despite contrary evidence.[^evidence]

## Conditions and limits

General patterns do not replace local diagnosis. Situation-specific data can be weak, incomplete, or misleading, while established knowledge may not apply unchanged to every context.[^evidence]

## Decision implications

An agent should distinguish general evidence, local evidence, and untested assumptions. It should state what would change the recommendation and avoid treating correlation or custom as proof.

# Customer needs and market research

## Core statement

Understanding customer needs and market conditions helps organisations identify opportunities, design relevant offers, and avoid wasteful decisions. Current customer knowledge is required because needs, preferences, competitors, and market opportunities change.[^needs]

## Mechanism and relationships

Market research gathers evidence about buyers, usage, choices, and unmet needs. Customer-oriented thinking then carries this evidence into product development, service design, communication, and broader organisational decisions. Competitors can replace organisations that fail to update their understanding of customers.[^needs]

## Evidence and data

The chapter describes research into customer choices and price perceptions before market entry, and uses service examples to show the importance of understanding the perspective of the people served.[^needs]

## Conditions and limits

Customer statements are not the only evidence of need; actual behaviour, constraints, and future market conditions also matter. Research should inform decisions, not simply confirm a preferred solution.[^needs]

## Decision implications

An agent should define the customer question, identify the relevant evidence, separate reported wants from observed behaviour, and show how the findings change the offer or decision.

# Marketing metrics and market-based assets

## Core statement

Marketing metrics track brand performance and the effects of marketing interventions. Mental availability and physical availability are market-based assets that make a brand easier to buy and influence both current and future sales.[^assets]

## Definitions

Mental availability is the likelihood that a buyer notices, recognises, or recalls a brand in a buying situation. Physical availability is the ease of finding and buying the brand. Marketing interventions include changes to product, price, promotion, and place.[^assets]

## Mechanism and relationships

An intervention can generate immediate sales while also building or damaging availability assets that affect future sales. Metrics should therefore monitor both short-term outcomes and the underlying assets through which marketing activity works.[^assets]

## Evidence and data

The chapter defines the four marketing-mix elements and presents mental and physical availability as the two principal market-based assets. It warns that pursuing temporary sales without measurement can damage these assets.[^assets]

## Conditions and limits

Sales alone do not reveal whether an intervention strengthened future purchasing ease or merely shifted timing or price. Asset metrics must be interpreted alongside market conditions and buyer behaviour.[^assets]

## Decision implications

An agent should evaluate marketing action through both immediate outcomes and its likely effect on mental and physical availability, using measures that match each mechanism.

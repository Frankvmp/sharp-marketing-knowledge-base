# Sustainable marketing

## Core statement

Sustainable marketing evaluates current revenue opportunities alongside long-term risks to the business model, customer demand, and broader society. It rejects the assumption that meeting current demand alone is sufficient for long-term marketing success.[^sustainable]

## Mechanism and relationships

A marketing action can produce revenue today while weakening future demand, trust, access to resources, or social acceptance. Sustainable marketing therefore considers the durability of the offer, the organisation’s ability to operate, and the long-term consequences of satisfying current wants.[^sustainable]

## Evidence and data

The chapter defines sustainable marketing as ongoing analysis of long-term revenues and risks and discusses products whose adverse side effects can create broader social and market consequences.[^sustainable]

## Conditions and limits

Long-term sustainability cannot be established merely by a brand claim or a short-lived sales result. The relevant risks and time horizons must be specified, including who bears the costs and how demand may change.[^sustainable]

## Decision implications

An agent should state the short-term benefit, the long-term risks, the affected groups, and the evidence needed to judge whether a marketing decision is sustainable.

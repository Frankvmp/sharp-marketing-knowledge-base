# Repeat buying and limited search

## Core statement

Most buying is repeat behaviour. Buyers commonly return to categories, brands, and stores they have used before, using habit and convenience to avoid extensive evaluation on every occasion.[^repeat]

## Mechanism

Large choice sets make continual comparison costly in time and attention. Familiar options reduce that burden. The resulting personal repertoire constrains the brands or stores considered on a purchase occasion, even where buyers are not experts and alternatives are abundant.[^repeat]

## Evidence and data

The cited car-purchase evidence reports repeat purchase close to 50 per cent and consideration of about two brands on average, despite roughly 60 available brands and long intervals between purchases. A study of more than 10,000 internet households found active-month visits averaging 1.2 book sites, 1.3 CD sites, and 1.8 travel sites. Similar limited search was reported for retail banking and insurance.[^repeat]

## Conditions and limits

First category purchases, hobby-related purchases, and choices with substantial consequences can receive more attention. Yet observed behaviour in potentially high-involvement categories still often shows repeat purchase and limited search. Greater involvement is therefore not proof of extensive comparison.[^involvement]

## Decision use

Assess whether the brand is already in the buyer's repertoire and is easy to retrieve and buy. Do not design on the assumption that buyers will compare every available alternative.

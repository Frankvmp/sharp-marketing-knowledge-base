# Mental availability in choice situations

## Core statement

A brand must be recalled or noticed in a relevant choice situation to be purchased. Mental availability is the probability that the brand is easy to notice, recognise, or retrieve from memory in those circumstances.[^memory]

## Definitions

- **Choice situation** is the time and place where a purchase decision occurs.
- **Memory association** is information linked to a brand, such as category, occasion, colour, character, or use context.
- **Share of mind** is the quantity and quality of associations connected to a brand.[^memory]

## Mechanism

Some buying situations require recall before search or store choice. Others present brands visually, making recognition and noticing important. In both, memory matters. Associations formed and refreshed through use, advertising, word of mouth, and observation increase the probability that the brand is retrieved across relevant situations.[^memory]

## Evidence and data

Memory is described as probabilistic, not fixed: recall, recognition, and noticing vary with context, time, mood, and what has just been encountered. Larger and fresher association networks increase the chance that a brand comes to mind. The source uses associative networks to describe how category-entry cues can activate brand memory.[^memory]

## Conditions and limits

A high general awareness score does not prove retrieval in every choice situation. Memory also decays and faces interference from competing information and advertising. The goal is not universal recall but a higher probability of retrieval where the brand can be bought.[^forgetting]

## Decision use

1. Identify relevant choice situations.
2. Identify cues that currently bring the brand to mind or fail to do so.
3. Build and refresh associations that link the brand to those cues.
4. Ensure physical availability can convert recall into purchase.

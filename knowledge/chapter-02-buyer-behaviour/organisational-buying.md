# Organisational buying

## Core statement

Important organisational purchases can involve several people, multiple departments, supplier meetings, technical specialists, negotiation, and long decision periods. Complexity is justified when the purchase is strategically important or high value.[^b2b]

## Mechanism

A buying centre brings together people with different requirements and roles. The decision process evaluates customer requirements and supplier capability, then may continue through negotiations before a supply contract is agreed. Organisational characteristics such as industry, size, and operating geography can affect purchasing requirements and behaviour.[^b2b]

## Conditions and limits

Business buying is not uniformly slow or complex. Complexity depends on the value, strategic importance, risk, and technical requirements of the purchase. Personal relationships can influence organisational decisions, but should not be treated as the only driver.[^b2b]

## Decision use

Identify the decision-making unit, each role's requirements, the purchase risks, and the expected process before planning a business-market intervention. Do not generalise consumer purchase patterns directly to a high-value organisational buying context.

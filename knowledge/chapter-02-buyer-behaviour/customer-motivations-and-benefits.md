# Customer motivations and benefits

## Core statement

Buyers purchase brands as routes to the benefits offered by a product or service category. Marketing must distinguish category benefits from product features and recognise that basic motivations become different wants across people, cultures, and situations.[^motivation]

## Mechanism

Features matter when they deliver benefits relevant to buyer needs. A feature may need to be translated and explained so that buyers can connect it to the benefit. Directly asking buyers why they made small or repeated purchases is limited because reasons can be idiosyncratic, forgotten, or reconstructed after the event.[^motivation]

## Framework and limit

A hierarchy of motivations can remind practitioners of multiple human needs, but it has low predictive and explanatory value for specific behaviour because real behaviour is complex and situation-dependent. Basic motivations may be shared while wants differ between demographic groups, cultures, countries, and contexts.[^motivation]

## Decision use

Define the category benefit that the offer enables. Then test whether the feature, communication, price, and availability make that benefit credible and usable in the relevant situation. Do not treat a general motivation framework as a precise segmentation or prediction model.

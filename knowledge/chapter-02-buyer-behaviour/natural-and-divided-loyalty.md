# Natural and divided loyalty

## Core statement

Behavioural loyalty is widespread and can form without meaningful product differences, advertising, packaging differences, or superior physical availability. It is rarely exclusive: buyers generally divide purchases across several brands in a personal repertoire.[^loyalty]

## Definitions

- **Behavioural loyalty** is repeated purchase from a relatively stable personal repertoire.
- **Divided or polygamous loyalty** is buying more than one brand in the category.
- **Exclusive loyalty** means buying only one brand within the observation period.[^loyalty]

## Mechanism

Loyalty simplifies choice, reduces effort, and lowers perceived risk. It does not necessarily signal strong emotional attachment, a belief that one brand is uniquely superior, or exclusive purchasing. Repertoire structure means a buyer can have one dominant brand while still using other brands or stores.[^loyalty][^prosaic]

## Evidence and data

Evidence from an experiment with identically produced and packaged bread found that households quickly developed loyalty patterns resembling real-world repeat buying. The cited brand table reports that exclusively loyal buyers were a minority, averaging 11 per cent across its selected brands. The double-jeopardy pattern is presented as showing larger differences in buyer numbers than in buyer loyalty between large and small brands.[^loyalty]

## Conditions and limits

The pattern concerns observed purchase, not a claim that all buyers have identical motives. Loyalty can be stronger for a dominant store or brand, but divided purchasing remains normal. Loyalty-program participation is not itself evidence of incremental loyalty because heavy existing buyers are more likely to join.[^prosaic]

## Decision use

Do not make exclusive loyalty the default objective. Evaluate whether the brand is included in relevant repertoires and whether a proposed intervention increases incremental buying rather than simply rewarding existing heavy buyers.

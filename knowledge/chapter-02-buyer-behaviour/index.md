
# Chapter 2: Buyer behaviour

## Scope

This chapter explains how buyer behaviour is observed and interpreted, with emphasis on repeat buying, limited repertoires, memory, decision shortcuts, buyer differences, purchase journeys, attitudes, and organisational buying.[^source]

## Concept map

- [Descriptive buyer evidence](./descriptive-buyer-evidence.md) — Buyer evidence records what current and potential buyers do and is interpreted using established behavioural patterns.
- [Repeat buying and limited search](./repeat-buying-and-limited-search.md) — Most purchases rely on familiar options, resulting in repeat behaviour and limited comparison even in some consequential categories.
- [Natural and divided loyalty](./natural-and-divided-loyalty.md) — Behavioural loyalty forms readily, but is usually divided across a personal repertoire rather than exclusive to one brand.
- [Light buyers and buying frequencies](./light-buyers-and-buying-frequencies.md) — Brand and category purchasing is typically skewed: a small number of heavy buyers coexist with many light buyers.
- [Mental availability in choice situations](./mental-availability-in-choice-situations.md) — The probability that a brand is noticed, recognised, or recalled depends on accessible memory associations in a specific buying situation.
- [Heuristics and non-conscious choice](./heuristics-and-non-conscious-choice.md) — Buyers use automatic and experience-based shortcuts to make workable decisions with limited time, attention, and information.
- [Customer motivations and benefits](./customer-motivations-and-benefits.md) — Buyers seek category benefits, while basic motivations are translated into culturally and situationally variable wants.
- [Buyer heterogeneity](./buyer-heterogeneity.md) — Buyers differ in repertoires, circumstances, and response to marketing; only actionable differences should shape marketing choices.
- [Purchase-journey diagnosis](./purchase-journey-diagnosis.md) — A customer journey maps the category-specific stages through which buyers move and identifies where conversion is lost.
- [Attitudes, intentions, and buying behaviour](./attitudes-intentions-and-buying-behaviour.md) — Brand attitudes and stated intentions often reflect past behaviour and have limited power to predict or change future buying on their own.
- [Organisational buying](./organisational-buying.md) — Business buying can involve multiple decision-makers, technical requirements, negotiations, and strategic purchasing contexts.

## Coverage rule

A source section is complete only when its important concepts, evidence, conditions, and decision relevance are represented in linked records. A case study remains supporting evidence unless it provides a distinct reusable mechanism.

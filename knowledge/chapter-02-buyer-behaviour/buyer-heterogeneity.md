# Buyer heterogeneity

## Core statement

Buyers differ in their category purchase rates, repertoires, preferences, memories, circumstances, and access. Similar demographic characteristics do not guarantee similar brand choices, so a single persona can conceal important variation.[^heterogeneity]

## Mechanism

Different buyers build different memory structures through many past experiences. Differences become strategically useful when they affect how buyers respond to marketing, can be reached, or can obtain the offer. Geographic location, language, life stage, media use, and business characteristics can matter when they change communication, distribution, or service requirements.[^heterogeneity]

## Conditions and limits

Not every observed difference is actionable. Hair colour could matter for hair dye but not for most offers. Segmentation should identify differences that affect response to marketing, not merely construct a vivid profile. Product variety can serve heterogeneous preferences without relying on a single homogeneous target group.[^heterogeneity]

## Decision use

1. Identify buyer differences relevant to response or access.
2. Test whether the difference is connected to a practical marketing action.
3. Preserve awareness of variation within the chosen group.
4. Compare a brand's buyer profile with the category profile before treating a difference as a brand-specific opportunity.

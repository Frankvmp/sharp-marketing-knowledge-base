# Descriptive buyer evidence

## Core statement

Buyer understanding begins with descriptive evidence about who buys, where, when, how much, what they buy, what they watch, and how the category fits their lives. The observation becomes useful when interpreted against established recurring patterns rather than internal intuition.[^evidence]

## Definitions

- **Descriptive data** records observable characteristics or behaviour of current and potential buyers.
- **Empirical research** uses direct observation or data from the real world.
- **Behavioural pattern** is a recurring relationship used to judge whether an observation is ordinary, unusual, or decision-relevant.[^evidence]

## Reasoning procedure

1. Define the buyer decision or behaviour to be understood.
2. Gather evidence about actual and potential buyers.
3. Select the relevant established pattern for the category, brand, store, or business purchase.
4. Compare the observation with that pattern.
5. Keep executive or colleague opinion separate from observed evidence.[^evidence]

## Conditions and limits

Marketing personnel have a different relationship with a brand from buyers because the brand is part of their work and income. Their experience can generate hypotheses but cannot substitute for buyer evidence. Patterns support interpretation; they do not remove the need to examine the specific situation.[^evidence]

## Decision use

Use this record before diagnosing loyalty, targeting, media, availability, or customer attitudes. Do not infer buyer priorities from internal enthusiasm for the brand.

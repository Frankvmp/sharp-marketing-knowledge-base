# Light buyers and buying frequencies

## Core statement

Buyer populations are typically skewed: a few buyers purchase heavily while many buy infrequently. Most buyers therefore purchase an individual brand below its average rate, making the brand a small part of most customers' lives.[^frequency]

## Definitions

- **Light buyer** buys the category or brand less frequently than the average.
- **Buying-frequency distribution** describes how purchases are distributed across buyers over a period.
- **Category buying** and **brand buying** must be separated because buyers divide category purchases across their repertoires.[^frequency]

## Mechanism

Most brand buyers are light for two linked reasons. Category buying itself is usually skewed, with many people buying the category only occasionally. Each category purchase is then divided among several brands, so even heavy category buyers can be light buyers of any individual brand.[^frequency]

## Evidence and data

The source describes a skewed statistical distribution, with a small number of heavy buyers and many light buyers. It uses breakfast-cereal purchase data to illustrate skewed category buying and reports that the concentration of light buyers is normal even for large brands.[^frequency]

## Conditions and limits

A high purchase frequency for a small group does not imply that most buyers purchase frequently. An agent should not generalise heavy-buyer behaviour to the whole customer base or mistake average purchase rate for the typical buyer's rate.[^frequency]

## Decision use

Build and maintain attention and availability for the broad buyer base, including infrequent buyers. Do not rely solely on relationship activity aimed at the most frequent buyers when the objective is broad market growth.

# Attitudes, intentions, and buying behaviour

## Core statement

Brand attitudes and intentions have a weak independent influence on behaviour change. They often reflect past buying, familiarity, and existing loyalty, while purchase behaviour is also shaped by memory, availability, competing goals, and the immediate situation.[^attitudes]

## Mechanism

A buyer can hold a positive attitude yet fail to remember it at the relevant moment, face competing goals, or encounter a different context. Brands with more users tend to score higher on attitudinal measures because users are more familiar with them and more likely to say positive things. Intentions similarly tend to reflect what buyers have previously done, rather than accurately anticipate a change in behaviour.[^attitudes]

## Evidence and limits

Attitudinal responses are probabilistic: buyers can give different responses over time, often favouring brands in their repertoire and especially brands they buy more often. An attitude can reinforce existing behaviour, and an intention can matter in some behaviour changes, but neither should be treated as a sufficient cause of buying.[^attitudes][^conclusion]

## Decision use

When the objective is behaviour change, diagnose situational and environmental barriers alongside attitudes. Prioritise mental and physical availability, and use attitudes or intentions as contextual evidence rather than as a stand-alone prediction of future demand.

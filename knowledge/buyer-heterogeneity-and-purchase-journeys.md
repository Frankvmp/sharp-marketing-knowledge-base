# Buyer heterogeneity and purchase journeys

## Core statement

Buyers differ in their repertoires, purchase rates, memories, locations, circumstances, and media use. Marketing should identify differences that affect response or access without representing the market as a single homogeneous buyer profile.[^heterogeneity]

## Mechanism and relationships

Even people with similar demographic characteristics can have different brand repertoires and loyalties because their memory structures and past experiences differ. Some differences are actionable because they change how buyers can be reached, what language or content they need, or where the brand must be available. Other differences are irrelevant to the category and should not drive segmentation.[^heterogeneity]

Buyers also differ in where they are on the path to a category purchase. A journey map can diagnose whether a problem is awareness, consideration, access, conversion, or repurchase. The stages must be derived for the specific product, market, and buyer group rather than copied from a generic funnel.[^journey]

## Evidence and data requirements

Customer and category data should identify demographic, geographic, cultural, behavioural, and business characteristics that affect response to marketing. Compare each brand's buyer profile with the category profile, then test whether observed differences indicate a reach, availability, or communication problem.

For a purchase journey, define the actual stages and sequence, measure conversion from one stage to the next, and compare the brand's conversion with the relevant category benchmark.[^journey]

## Decision procedure

1. List the buyer differences that could affect reach, response, need, or physical availability.
2. Exclude characteristics with no plausible connection to the category decision.
3. Avoid a single persona that hides variation within current and potential buyers.
4. Define the specific journey stages for the category and buyer group.
5. Measure conversion between stages and locate the largest decision-relevant loss.
6. Select the intervention that addresses that specific loss.

## Conditions and limits

A standard funnel can be a useful diagnostic reminder, but it is not a valid ready-made model for every category. The stages for a new car, a chocolate bar, experienced professional buyers, and newer buyers can differ substantially. A buyer profile is descriptive, not proof that every buyer behaves in the same way.[^journey]

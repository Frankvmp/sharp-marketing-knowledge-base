# Marketing mix

## Core statement

The marketing mix, or 4Ps, is the set of manageable elements in a brand's marketing plan that is adjusted to implement marketing strategy: product, price, promotion, and place. The mix should be consistent so it does not confuse buyers.[^sharp-2017-ch1-mix]

## Framework elements

| Element | Chapter meaning | Questions for an agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product | The product or service and its features | What customer need is being addressed, and is there a market for it? |
| Price | What is charged and how payment is structured | Does the price reflect costs, competitive context, and what buyers may pay? |
| Promotion | Advertising and communication | Who needs to be reached, how, where, and how often? |
| Place | Distribution and physical availability | Where, through which outlet types, and by which channels can buyers obtain the offering? |

## Operational detail

Product decisions cover the need to meet, the existence of a market, the intended market, and the intended buyers. Price decisions consider production cost, competitor prices, margins, payment methods, and regulation. Promotion decisions consider the audience, message, media, frequency, and the offering's distribution. Place decisions consider outlet type, geography, online versus in-store access, and the extent of availability.[^sharp-2017-ch1-mix]

## Reasoning rule

Treat the four elements as connected. A decision in one element may make another less coherent or less effective. For example, the chapter notes that promoting an offering where it cannot be bought wastes resources, so promotion and distribution should be checked together.[^sharp-2017-ch1-mix]

## Scope and limits

The 4Ps are a framework for organising decisions. They are not an instruction to make frequent changes, nor evidence that a change will improve performance. Use [marketing metrics](./marketing-metrics.md) and [market-based assets](./market-based-assets.md) to evaluate proposed interventions.[^sharp-2017-ch1-mix]

# Marketing implementation and control

## Core statement

Implementation makes strategy operational through assigned activities, resources, timing, and coordination. Control compares outcomes with objectives, diagnoses deviations, and informs the next planning cycle.[^implementation]

## Definitions

Implementation is the conversion of a plan into coordinated work. Marketing control is the process of reviewing performance against intended outcomes and deciding whether action or the plan needs adjustment.[^implementation]

## Mechanism and relationships

A usable plan identifies what will be done, by whom, when, with what resources, and how progress will be measured. It coordinates marketing activity with organisational structures, people, budgets, and systems. Control then creates feedback between implementation results and future planning.[^implementation]

## Evidence and data

The chapter describes implementation and control as essential sections of a marketing plan. It frames control as part of the planning cycle, not a separate post-hoc reporting activity.[^implementation]

## Control sequence

1. Set standards of performance.
2. Identify tools for measuring market progress.
3. Evaluate actual performance against the marketing objectives.
4. Take corrective action when necessary.[^implementation]

## Conditions and limits

A plan can be analytically sound while failing in execution. Control data must be interpreted against the original objective and market circumstances, rather than treated as a standalone score.[^implementation]

## Decision implications

An agent should not recommend a strategy without defining ownership, resources, timing, measures, and a review mechanism. A material performance gap should trigger diagnosis before merely changing tactics.

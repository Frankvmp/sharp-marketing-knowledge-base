# Marketing planning cycle

## Core statement

Marketing planning uses a structured cycle to assess market circumstances, select objectives and strategy, implement activity, and control results. The process supports decisions about market entry, exit, opportunity, and resource allocation.[^cycle]

## Definitions

A marketing plan states the intended direction, activities, resources, responsibilities, and controls for marketing activity. Control compares outcomes with expectations and turns the result into learning for the next cycle.[^cycle]

## Mechanism and relationships

1. Analyse the market environment, competitors, and the organisation’s internal capabilities.
2. Analyse target markets, buyer needs and wants, buying behaviour, segmentation, and positioning.
3. Set marketing objectives and formulate the strategy and marketing-mix decisions needed to achieve them.
4. Implement the strategy and control progress against the marketing objectives, using the resulting learning in the next cycle.[^cycle]

## Evidence and data

The chapter presents planning as a recurring managerial process and illustrates the content normally included in a marketing plan. It does not claim that a formal plan alone causes performance.[^cycle]

## Conditions and limits

The value of planning depends on using the plan as a living process of implementation and learning rather than as a static document.[^cycle]

## Decision implications

An agent should require an explicit link between situation analysis, target-market understanding, strategic choices, assigned action, and feedback before treating a marketing plan as actionable.

# Ethical decision frameworks

## Core statement

Ethical decisions can be examined through multiple frameworks because each highlights a different dimension of a problem. Consequence-based reasoning considers benefit and harm; duty-based reasoning considers universal obligations; virtue ethics considers the character and habits the organisation seeks to embody.[^frameworks]

## Definitions

Teleology judges actions by their outcomes. Utilitarianism is a teleological approach that seeks the greatest good for the greatest number. Deontology gives priority to duties and universal standards regardless of outcome. Virtue ethics focuses on the qualities and values expressed through habitual action.[^frameworks]

## Mechanism and relationships

A structured ethical process first develops awareness, identifies the ethical issue and stakeholders, considers relevant principles and alternatives, makes and justifies a decision, then monitors the outcome. Using several ethical perspectives expands the range of consequences and obligations considered before action.[^frameworks]

## Seven-step ethical decision process

1. Cultivate ethical awareness and sensitivity.
2. Identify the ethical issues or questions.
3. Identify stakeholders in the decision, including their interests, potential benefit or harm, and influence.
4. Select an ethical theory or standard through which to examine the decision.
5. Specify alternatives or ethical analyses.
6. Make and justify a decision.
7. Monitor outcomes for intended effects and adverse ethical consequences.[^frameworks]

## Evidence and data

The chapter presents teleology, deontology, and virtue ethics as complementary perspectives and outlines a seven-step process that ends with monitoring the decision’s outcomes.[^frameworks]

## Conditions and limits

The frameworks can produce conflicting recommendations and do not remove the need for judgement. A formal process is weakened if organisational incentives discourage employees from raising ethical concerns.[^frameworks]

## Decision implications

An agent should make the ethical basis of a recommendation explicit: identify consequences, duties, and relevant organisational values; document alternatives; then specify how actual outcomes will be monitored.

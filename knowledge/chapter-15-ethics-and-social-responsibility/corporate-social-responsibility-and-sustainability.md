# Corporate social responsibility and sustainability

## Core statement

Corporate social responsibility connects commercial activity with duties to people, society, and the environment. Sustainability requires meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.[^csr]

## Definitions

Corporate social responsibility includes economic responsibility, legal compliance, and responsibilities that go beyond minimum law. Cause-related marketing links a purchase to a social contribution. The triple bottom line assesses economic, social, and environmental objectives together.[^csr]

## Mechanism and relationships

Economic success provides employment, investment, taxes, and return to owners. Legal compliance establishes minimum rules. Corporate responsibility considers wider impacts, including supply-chain conditions, community effects, environmental limits, and the credibility of public commitments. The triple bottom line makes these dimensions part of performance assessment.[^csr]

## Evidence and data

The chapter distinguishes philanthropy from cause-related marketing and describes corporate responsibility initiatives that link social and environmental outcomes with company operations. It presents the triple bottom line as a way of evaluating economic, social, and environmental performance.[^csr]

## Conditions and limits

A social initiative can be a marketing tactic rather than a meaningful responsibility commitment. Claims of responsibility require evidence about outcomes, not only statements of intent or charitable association.[^csr]

## Decision implications

An agent should distinguish legal compliance, philanthropy, cause-related marketing, and operational CSR. It should require measurable economic, social, and environmental outcomes before characterising a programme as sustainable or responsible.

# Ethical risks in the marketing mix

## Core statement

Every marketing-mix element can create ethical risk. Product choices raise safety, sustainability, packaging, and labelling issues; pricing can involve transparency, predation, or collusion; promotion can mislead or exploit; and distribution can abuse power or restrict fair access.[^mix]

## Definitions

Predatory pricing uses sustained low prices to weaken competitors. Price fixing is coordination among firms to control prices. Stealth marketing conceals the sponsoring relationship behind a marketing message. Ethical supply-chain management requires fair dealing and responsible use of power across buyers and sellers.[^mix]

## Mechanism and relationships

Short-term commercial pressure can encourage claims, price, channel, or promotion choices that transfer cost or harm to consumers, suppliers, competitors, or society. Reputational loss, regulation, recalls, compensation, reduced trust, and lost business can then follow.[^mix]

## Evidence and data

The chapter gives examples involving product safety, misleading labelling and claims, price wars and price fixing, exploitation in supply chains, personal selling, and undisclosed promotional activity.[^mix]

## Conditions and limits

A practice can benefit some consumers while harming others or damaging future competition. Ethical risk assessment therefore needs to consider distribution of effects, not only average or short-term consumer benefit.[^mix]

## Decision implications

An agent should assess product, price, promotion, and physical availability separately. It should require accurate claims, safe products, transparent sponsorship, fair dealing, and a defensible account of effects on consumers, suppliers, and competition.

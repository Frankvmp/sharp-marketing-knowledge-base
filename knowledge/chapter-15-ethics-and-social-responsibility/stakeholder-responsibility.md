# Stakeholder responsibility

## Core statement

A company has responsibilities to groups affected by its activities, not only to shareholders. Stakeholders can also influence company outcomes, so marketing decisions require identifying their interests, power, and potential conflicts.[^stakeholders]

## Definitions

Stakeholders include internal groups such as employees and shareholders, and external groups such as customers, suppliers, communities, governments, regulators, industry bodies, and media. Stakeholder mapping compares a group’s interest in a decision with its power to influence that decision.[^stakeholders]

## Mechanism and relationships

High-interest, high-power stakeholders require close management. High-interest, low-power stakeholders should be kept informed. Low-interest, high-power stakeholders should be kept satisfied. Low-interest, low-power stakeholders need proportionate attention. Mapping does not resolve conflicts; it makes them visible for a justified decision.[^stakeholders]

## Evidence and data

The chapter describes competing interests among shareholders, employees, suppliers, communities, government, and customers. It presents an interest–power grid as a practical way to select an appropriate stakeholder response.[^stakeholders]

## Conditions and limits

Power and interest can change during a project or after public attention, regulatory action, or market events. Mapping must not reduce affected people to a tool for managing reputational risk.[^stakeholders]

## Decision implications

An agent should map who is affected and who can influence the outcome before recommending a contentious action. It should surface conflicting interests rather than assuming shareholder value is the only decision criterion.

# Environmental scanning, SWOT, and ETOP

## Core statement

Environmental information supports both planning and control: it informs assumptions about future conditions and helps diagnose deviations from a marketing plan. Firms must understand, respond to, and adapt to material change rather than relying on past operating conditions.[^monitoring]

## Process

1. Scan the micro- and macro-environment for changes relevant to demand, costs, access, legitimacy, and competitive position.
2. Summarise internal strengths and weaknesses alongside external opportunities and threats in a SWOT analysis.
3. Assess opportunities by attractiveness and probability of success.
4. Assess threats by seriousness and probability of occurrence.
5. Use an Environmental Threat and Opportunity Profile (ETOP) to summarise the most critical factors and stimulate senior-management discussion.[^monitoring][^swot]

## Definitions

- **SWOT:** an assessment of strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats.
- **Opportunity attractiveness:** potential market size, growth, margins, competition, channels, technical requirements, restrictions, grants, ecological and energy considerations.
- **Success probability:** the likelihood that internal resources and advantage can capture an opportunity.
- **ETOP:** a summary of critical environmental factors classified as opportunities or threats, often with probability estimates.[^swot]

## Conditions and limits

SWOT and ETOP organise judgement; they do not predict the future. A threat matrix requires explicit assumptions about probability and seriousness, and an apparent opportunity still requires a fit with finance, capability, distribution, production, and managerial competence.[^swot]

## Decision implications

Use the frameworks to expose assumptions and decide where monitoring or preparation is justified. Do not equate a long environmental-factor list with strategy; connect each material factor to a decision, threshold, owner, and response option.

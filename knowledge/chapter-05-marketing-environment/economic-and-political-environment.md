# Economic and political environment

## Core statement

Economic conditions affect customers' willingness and ability to buy, while political systems affect how firms may operate, compete, trade, employ, and communicate. Both must be monitored because national and international changes can transmit rapidly across markets.[^economic][^political]

## Economic indicators

Relevant economic signals include gross domestic product, inflation, savings ratios, income distribution, exchange rates, industry demand, and international trade conditions. A change in a large economy can alter export demand, prices, employment, and local expenditure through interconnected markets.[^economic]

## Political constraints and access

Political analysis must cover local, regional, national, and international levels. Regulation protects consumers, competition, and wider societal interests; international agreements, trade blocs, and intergovernmental organisations can also alter market access and operating conditions.[^political]

## Conditions and limits

Economic and political outcomes are difficult to control and can be difficult to predict. A macro trend can create both threat and opportunity: reduced demand in one sector may coincide with new opportunities in another. General economic health does not reveal the effect on every buyer group or region.[^economic]

## Decision implications

Maintain explicit assumptions about demand, cost, access, and regulation. Link each assumption to observable indicators and specify which marketing decision would change if the indicator moves.

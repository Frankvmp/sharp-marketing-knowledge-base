# Publics, value chains, and dependencies

## Core statement

Publics are groups with a direct or indirect interest in, or influence on, organisational objectives. Value chains show how suppliers, intermediaries, and service providers progressively transform inputs into a customer-valued offer.[^publics][^value-chain]

## Stakeholder categories

Relevant publics can include government, the financial community, pressure groups, local communities, industry bodies, investors, and other groups seeking influence. Government affects policy, regulation, taxes, charges, funding, and market conditions; firms may also seek to influence government outcomes through legitimate public-relations and lobbying activity.[^publics][^government]

## Value-chain reasoning

Value is created across connected activities rather than only at the final sale. Components, processing, distribution, retail access, service, and convenience can each add value. The customer's perceived price and value need not reflect each participant's underlying costs.[^value-chain]

## Conditions and limits

Organisations depend on external capabilities for production, distribution, legal services, advertising, finance, and other activities. Community support can improve legitimacy and reputation only when perceived as genuine; it does not remove the need to comply with regulation or address substantive impacts.[^community][^dependencies]

## Decision implications

Identify stakeholders that can affect access, legitimacy, finance, regulation, and customer value. Trace the value chain to locate dependencies and distinguish which partners create customer value, which impose risk, and where relationship failure can damage the offer.

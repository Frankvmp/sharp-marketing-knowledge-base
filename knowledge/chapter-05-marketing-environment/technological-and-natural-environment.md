# Technological and natural environment

## Core statement

Technological change can change products, information search, communication, distribution, and industry structure. Natural-resource constraints and environmental concern affect both corporate strategy and the acceptability of marketing choices.[^technology][^natural]

## Technological reasoning

Assess technology as new product capability, communication infrastructure, data capability, and potential disruption to existing industry models. Digital change can alter how buyers search, interact, and buy, but traditional technologies can remain relevant to niche or subcultural markets.[^technology]

## Natural-environment reasoning

Assess dependence on finite or vulnerable resources, environmental effects of operations, regulatory exposure, and the relevance of sustainability, waste, pollution, and ecological damage to buyer decisions. Environmental concern can extend across market segments rather than belonging only to a specialised green segment.[^natural]

## Conditions and limits

New technology is not automatically valuable to buyers or the firm, and past market success does not protect an incumbent from disruption. Environmental positioning cannot substitute for responsible corporate practices where the offer depends on natural resources or generates material harm.[^technology][^natural]

## Decision implications

Maintain a technology-and-resource watchlist linked to customer behaviour, operational dependencies, and likely regulation. Test whether a change modifies the buying process, the value delivered, or the legitimacy of the offer before changing tactics.

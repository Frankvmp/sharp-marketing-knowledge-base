# Micro-environment relationships

## Core statement

The micro-environment consists of the organisations and people with which an organisation interacts directly. It includes the organisation itself, customers, competitors, intermediaries, suppliers, and publics; the organisation can influence, but not fully control, many of these relationships.[^micro]

## Relationship logic

Internal resources, leadership, structure, and relationships between functions determine what marketing actions are feasible. Customer needs and preferences require continuing evidence and segmentation rather than assumptions. Competitor analysis must define competition by the customer need or market served, not only by similar products.[^organisation][^customers][^competitors]

## Intermediaries and suppliers

Intermediaries transfer products and ownership through distribution, retail, finance, and legal processes. Their supply speed, reliability, and service can shape the customer's assessment of the brand. Suppliers influence the quality, cost, reliability, and compliance of the final offer. These relationships require mutual benefit and active management, not a purely transactional view.[^intermediaries][^suppliers]

## Conditions and limits

A firm can influence direct relationships but cannot dictate the priorities or power of other actors. Retailer concentration can create unequal dependence between manufacturers and retailers, which changes bargaining power and feasible routes to market.[^power]

## Decision implications

Map every actor needed to create, distribute, communicate, and support the offer. For each relationship, identify the customer-value contribution, dependency, potential conflict, and the information needed to manage it.

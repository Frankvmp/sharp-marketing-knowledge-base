# Demographic and cultural environment

## Core statement

Demographic trends describe population size, structure, location, and characteristics; cultural trends shape social values, perceptions, and behaviour. Together they change market potential and the meanings buyers attach to offers.[^demography][^culture]

## Analysis procedure

1. Examine age structure, household composition, geographic distribution, gender balance, population growth, and spending capacity.
2. Identify changes that alter the size, accessibility, or viability of buyer groups.
3. Examine cultural values, institutions, social roles, history, and broader social pressures.
4. Connect demographic shifts to changing needs and cultural interpretations rather than treating population counts as a complete explanation.[^demography][^culture]

## Evidence and data

An ageing population can enlarge demand in some categories while reducing the viability of others. Growth in single-person households and geographic movement can change buying patterns and local service needs. The cultural meaning of ageing can also change: a larger older population alone does not explain demand for active, adventurous, or learning-oriented offers.[^demography][^culture]

## Conditions and limits

Demographic and cultural categories interact with economic, technological, and natural forces. A demographic segment should not be assumed viable merely because it exists, and advertising imagery should not be treated as a reliable description of household structure or market diversity.[^demography]

## Decision implications

Use population data to constrain market-size assumptions, then test how affected groups actually behave and what they value. Monitor cultural change continuously because opportunities and challenges can emerge before a trend becomes obvious in category data.

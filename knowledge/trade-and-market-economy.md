# Trade and market economy

## Core statement

Trade is the exchange of goods and services. Marketing systems connect sellers' offerings with buyers' needs and wants. A market economy is one in which consumer demand guides production and prices while privately owned businesses compete.[^sharp-2017-ch1-trade]

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Trade | Buying and selling goods and services |
| Specialisation | Expertise developed through focus on a particular skill or knowledge area |
| Marketing system | A network that matches sellers' products and services with buyers' needs and wants |
| Market economy | An economy where consumer demand guides production and prices and privately owned businesses compete |

## Mechanism and relationships

Trade enables specialisation: buyers obtain offerings they cannot efficiently produce themselves, while producers focus on particular capabilities. Marketing systems coordinate the connection between offerings and buyers. Marketing operates inside this wider system through choices about [marketing mix](./marketing-mix.md), availability, and communication.[^sharp-2017-ch1-trade]

## Evidence and data

The chapter uses historical and economic examples to frame trade and specialisation. These examples explain the system role of marketing; they do not provide a decision-ready estimate of the effects of a particular policy, market, or organisation.[^sharp-2017-ch1-trade]

## Conditions and limits

This concept describes the source's market-system framing. It does not establish that every market is competitive, that every trade outcome is beneficial, or that a particular organisation should enter a market without further evidence.

## Decision implications

An agent should identify the relevant buyers, offerings, alternatives, and routes to market before proposing marketing activity. It should distinguish an individual firm's marketing decision from broader policy or economic claims that need separate evidence.

# Social marketing evaluation

## Core statement

Evaluation is a required part of a social marketing initiative because it establishes whether the intended behaviour changed, informs stakeholders, and improves future interventions. Rigorous evaluation is particularly important when claims will face criticism or opposition.[^social-evaluation]

## Evaluation procedure

1. Define the target behaviour precisely.
2. Establish a pre-initiative benchmark for that behaviour.
3. Measure behaviour after the initiative and assess whether any change persists.
4. Include an unexposed control group where feasible, measured over the same periods.
5. Capture multiple behavioural measures and use mixed methods where possible.
6. Reduce error and bias in self-reported behaviour through suitable question design.
7. Record explanatory variables, participation, refusal, and contextual changes that help interpret results.[^social-evaluation][^evaluation-method]

## Conditions and limits

A post-initiative measure alone cannot establish success because there is no baseline. A behaviour change can reflect external conditions rather than the initiative. Multiple measures, controls, and careful self-report design increase confidence but do not guarantee certainty.[^evaluation-method]

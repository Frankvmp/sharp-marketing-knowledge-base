# Marketing executive role

## Core statement

Marketing executives make and recommend decisions about an organisation's offerings, intended markets, advertising, promotions, prices, and delivery partners. Their responsibilities include using metrics to assess brand health and marketing effects, and maintaining the mental and physical availability that supports sales.[^sharp-2017-ch1-role]

## Why this matters for agent reasoning

An agent using this knowledge should treat marketing as a connected decision system, not as a synonym for advertising. A recommendation about one marketing activity should be checked against its implications for the offer, buyers, price, communication, availability, and longer-term brand assets.[^sharp-2017-ch1-role]

## Decision model

| Element | Role in the chapter | Reasoning implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer insights and market research | Help marketers understand needs and opportunities | Use evidence about buyers rather than relying only on internal intuition |
| Market and financial data | Support analysis of market conditions and decision consequences | Distinguish observed data from estimates and judgement |
| Marketing decisions | Cover offering, target market, price, advertising, and distribution choices | Do not treat one choice in isolation from the rest of the marketing system |
| Marketing metrics | Assess brand health and the effect of actions | Use measurement to evaluate actions, not merely to report activity |
| Mental and physical availability | Market-based assets that underpin current and future sales | Test whether a proposal makes the brand easier to notice and buy |

## Evidence and operational detail

The decision scope includes choices about the offering, target market, price, advertising, promotion, and the agents or retail partners that deliver the offering. Customer insight and market research inform those choices; market and financial data support analysis; and metrics assess brand health and the effects of actions.[^sharp-2017-ch1-role]

## Conditions and limits

The role includes communication with management, staff, retailers, and consumers. It is broader than producing advertising or promotional material, but the chapter does not provide a complete job design or authority model.[^sharp-2017-ch1-role]

## Scope and limits

This is the chapter's role framing, not a complete operational procedure. It identifies decision areas and responsibilities, but does not itself determine the correct choice in a specific market.[^sharp-2017-ch1-role]

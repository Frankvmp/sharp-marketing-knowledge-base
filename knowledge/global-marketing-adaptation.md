# Global marketing adaptation

## Core statement

International expansion can provide growth beyond a saturated home market and production-scale efficiencies. It requires choices about the degree to which the marketing mix is standardised or adapted for each market.[^global-summary]

## Reasoning procedure

1. Assess whether foreign-market entry addresses constrained domestic growth or creates scale advantages.
2. Compare the cost savings of standardisation with the risk of poor market fit.
3. Assess sociocultural, technological, economic, political, and legal differences that affect the offer and marketing mix.
4. Choose how customer relationships and sales will be operated across countries, including resellers, local sales staff, home-market staff, and global account management where relevant.
5. Coordinate service and product compatibility for customers operating across markets.[^global-summary][^global-operations]

## Conditions and limits

Standardisation can reduce costs but can fail when buyer needs, institutions, or market conditions differ materially. Localisation is not automatically beneficial either; it requires a specific market reason and operational capability.[^global-summary]

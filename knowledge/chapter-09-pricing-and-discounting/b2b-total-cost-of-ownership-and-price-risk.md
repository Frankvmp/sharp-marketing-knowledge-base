# B2B total cost of ownership and price risk

## Core statement

Business buyers should evaluate price as part of total cost of ownership, not as an isolated purchase amount. A higher-priced offer can create lower total operating cost when it has a longer productive life, lower maintenance needs, more reliable supply, or lower interruption risk.[^b2b]

## Evaluation procedure

1. Define the purchase price and the operating period being considered.
2. Estimate maintenance, reliability, productive-life, and restart-cost consequences.
3. Identify the cost and operational effect of supply interruption.
4. Compare suppliers on total operating value, component quality, and reliability rather than initial price alone.
5. Test whether the offer's price and evidence of performance support the buyer's risk and cost requirements.[^b2b]

## Conditions and limits

A total-cost-of-ownership claim requires credible assumptions about usage, productive life, maintenance, and interruption costs. It does not justify a premium merely because an offer is more expensive or claims higher quality.[^b2b]

## Decision implications

For B2B pricing, make the full cost and risk logic visible to the buyer. Do not assume that a lower purchase price is the lower-cost option, or that a quality premium will be accepted without evidence of its operating value.

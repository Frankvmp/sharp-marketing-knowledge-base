# Costs, margins, and profit

## Core statement

Price directly affects revenue and profit. Cost-based pricing establishes a viability floor, but cost allocation and mark-up choices require judgement and do not reveal what buyers will pay.[^costs]

## Key distinctions

Fixed costs do not change directly with unit volume; variable costs do. Marginal cost pricing can accept lower prices for additional volume when revenue exceeds variable cost, while total profitability still depends on recovering fixed costs. Gross margin, net profit, and contribution must be interpreted against the relevant costs.[^costs][^margins]

## Decision implications

Use cost analysis to identify financial constraints and volume sensitivity. Do not treat a cost-plus calculation as evidence that the resulting price maximises profit or fits the market.

# Marketer roles

## Core statement

Marketing work includes an operational role that promotes and sells brands, produces or commissions marketing materials, organises promotions, and reports sales or research findings. It also includes a usually more senior role that makes informed marketing decisions and budget allocations, designs research and marketplace experiments, analyses data, and learns which strategies work better in particular situations.[^sharp-2017-ch1-roles]

## Definitions

| Role | Primary work |
| --- | --- |
| Operational marketer | Carries out recurring promotion, communication, coordination, and reporting activities |
| Marketing decision-maker | Selects actions and budgets using research, experiments, data analysis, and knowledge of market conditions |

## Mechanism and relationships

Operational work produces, executes, and reports marketing activity. Decision-making work determines which activity to pursue, why it is justified, and how resources are allocated. The decision-making role links directly to [evidence-based marketing](./evidence-based-marketing.md), [marketing metrics](./marketing-metrics.md), and the [marketing mix](./marketing-mix.md).[^sharp-2017-ch1-roles]

## Evidence and data

This distinction is a role classification presented in the chapter. It does not provide a quantitative distribution of job types, effectiveness estimates, or a universal organisational structure.[^sharp-2017-ch1-roles]

## Conditions and limits

The distinction is simplified: the source identifies the second role as usually more senior, rather than claiming every marketer fits only one category. Specific organisations may divide responsibilities differently.[^sharp-2017-ch1-roles]

## Decision implications

Before recommending action, identify whether the task is execution, decision-making, or both. A decision recommendation requires the relevant evidence, decision criteria, and budget or capability context; an execution task requires a defined decision to implement.

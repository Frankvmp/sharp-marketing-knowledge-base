# Market research process

## Core statement

Market research supports marketing decisions by turning an explicit decision problem into suitable evidence, analysis, and communication. Research quality depends on the fit between the question, design, evidence, analysis, and interpretation rather than on technological sophistication alone.[^research-process]

## Process

1. Define the business background, decision to be supported, and research objectives in a brief.
2. Review relevant secondary data and existing knowledge before commissioning new collection.
3. Choose qualitative or quantitative evidence, sampling, and research design that suit the objective.
4. Analyse the data with attention to sampling error, patterns, and uncertainty.
5. Report the findings, implications, and recommendations so that the intended reader can act on them.[^research-process]

## Evidence and interpretation

Business-to-business research may involve a small population of organisations, making a census possible. It can also require multiple respondents within a customer organisation because decisions, use, and service experience are distributed across roles. Internal records and prior research are important secondary sources, but they must still be assessed against the current objective.[^b2b-research]

A research report should give readers context, a clear explanation, and a summary. Its normal elements are an executive summary, background, objectives, method, findings, conclusions, implications, and recommendations. The purpose is not to include every analysis performed; it is to make the decision-relevant story understandable.[^reporting]

## Data presentation rules

Use whole numbers where sampling error makes decimal precision misleading. Sort tables by meaningful values, show totals where relevant, use averages or medians to reveal patterns, and state the key message of each table or chart. Select a format that makes the pattern visible rather than decorative.[^data-presentation]

## Conditions and limits

A technically advanced method is not automatically scientific, trustworthy, or useful. Research findings remain limited by their design, sampling, measures, data quality, and interpretation. A result should not be used to justify a decision when the link between the evidence and the decision is unspecified.[^research-process]

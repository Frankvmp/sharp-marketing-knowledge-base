# Marketing environment

## Core statement

The marketing environment consists of the individuals, organisations, and wider forces that influence buyer behaviour and an organisation's ability to serve its market. It must be monitored because changes can alter both opportunity and constraint.[^environment]

## Framework

- **Micro-environment:** actors with whom the organisation interacts directly, such as customers, competitors, suppliers, and government agencies. The organisation can influence some of these relationships.
- **Macro-environment:** wider forces outside direct organisational control, such as demographic trends, the financial system, political shifts, technology, culture, and the natural environment.[^environment]

## Reasoning procedure

1. Identify the relevant micro-environment actors and their immediate influence.
2. Identify macro-environment forces that could change future buyer needs, market access, costs, legitimacy, or competitor behaviour.
3. Map interactions: a technological, cultural, political, or environmental change can influence customers and direct partners together.
4. Assess which stakeholders' needs and expectations must be understood.
5. Adapt marketing choices to the changing environment while recognising what cannot be directly controlled.[^environment-conclusion]

## Conditions and limits

Environmental categories are analytical aids, not isolated realities. There is no clean boundary between technology and culture, or between external forces and customer behaviour. Sustainability, pollution, resource use, and environmental concern can shape broad market expectations rather than affecting only a specialised “green” segment.[^natural-environment]

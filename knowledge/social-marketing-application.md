# Social marketing application

## Core statement

Social marketing is an exchange process directed at behaviour or attitude change for social good rather than profit. It can be used by government and other organisations, and can operate at either a specific-brand level or a category-wide behaviour level.[^social-marketing]

## Planning questions

1. Define the target behaviour and the social outcome.
2. Decide whether the work promotes a particular organisation or a category-wide behaviour.
3. Identify the target market and the situation in which behaviour occurs.
4. Set behaviour, knowledge, and support objectives appropriate to voluntary change.
5. Apply the 7Ps in the social-marketing context.
6. Design broad reach and support mechanisms, then evaluate behavioural outcomes.[^social-marketing]

## Mechanism and conditions

Voluntary behaviour change requires buyers to understand the behaviour's importance and know what support is available. Knowledge objectives can address risks, benefits, barriers, and facilitators. Social marketing often requires broad reach through mass media as well as support for the requested change; communications alone are not sufficient where access or capability is missing.[^social-marketing][^knowledge]

## Ethical boundary

Corporate social marketing must consider stakeholder interests because organisational resources may be directed toward a chosen cause. A claimed social benefit does not remove the requirement to assess whose resources are used and who is affected.[^application]

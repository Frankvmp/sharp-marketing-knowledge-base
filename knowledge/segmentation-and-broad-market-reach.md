# Segmentation and broad market reach

## Core statement

Segmentation describes meaningful differences and similarities among buyers. It is useful when those differences affect response to marketing and can be acted on, but narrow targeting can exclude current and potential buyers. The strategic task is to serve a broad, heterogeneous market profitably while remaining mentally and physically available.[^segmentation]

## Definitions

- **Segmentation** divides a market into sub-groups with different buying behaviours or responses to marketing mixes.
- **Targeting** selects which buyers or segments the organisation will seek to serve.
- **Product variety** offers a portfolio of brands, variants, models, or stock-keeping units to accommodate meaningful preference differences.[^segmentation]

## Reasoning procedure

1. Identify buyer differences in behaviour, situation, need, access, or response.
2. Test whether each difference relates to identifiable characteristics that enable an actionable marketing response.
3. Assess organisational capability, profitable demand, and competitor activity.
4. Decide whether a segment-specific response, product variety, or broad reach best serves the market.
5. Preserve broad access and communications where they can include buyers without compromising the offer to any group.[^segmentation]

## Conditions and limits

Demographic groups may differ in category needs, but they often do not explain choice between competing brands. A portfolio of product variants can serve diverse preferences without assuming stable, internally homogeneous segments. A single “target consumer” persona can hide meaningful variation and reduce reach.[^segmentation][^product-variety]

## Decision implications

Avoid both extremes: a target so narrow that the organisation misses possible buyers, and a market so broad that it cannot serve it competitively. Evaluate how to be inclusive for buyers who may purchase occasionally, while preserving the product and price position required by the offer.[^segmentation-conclusion]

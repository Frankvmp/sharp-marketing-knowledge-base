# Customer needs and wants

## Core statement

Marketing helps an organisation understand customer needs, which are essential or very important, and how they translate into wants. Market research, observation, and surveys are important ways to develop customer insight.[^sharp-2017-ch1-customer]

Organisations can become absorbed in technical or internal concerns and lose sight of what customers need or want.[^sharp-2017-ch1-customer]

## Evidence and data

The chapter uses a hospital example to show how professional expertise can overlook customer fears and psychological needs. It also describes a McDonald's response to research indicating that health and food-quality concerns affected group decisions; the reported response combined menu changes, communication changes, store upgrades, and longer opening hours.[^sharp-2017-ch1-customer]

## Reasoning rule

When assessing a marketing decision, an agent should first ask whose needs are being served, what evidence supports that understanding, and whether the organisation is mistaking an internal preference for a customer priority.

## Evidence-to-decision sequence

1. Identify the relevant customer or buyer.
2. Separate observed needs, expressed wants, and internal assumptions.
3. Gather or assess available customer evidence through research, observation, or surveys.
4. Test whether the proposed offer and marketing mix respond to the evidence.
5. Check the longer-term consequences through [sustainable marketing](./sustainable-marketing.md).[^sharp-2017-ch1-customer]

## Scope and limits

The chapter presents customer understanding as essential, but does not say that every current customer desire should be fulfilled. That boundary is developed in [sustainable marketing](./sustainable-marketing.md).[^sharp-2017-ch1-customer]

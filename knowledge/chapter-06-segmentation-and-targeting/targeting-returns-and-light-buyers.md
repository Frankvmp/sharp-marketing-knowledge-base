# Targeting returns and light buyers

## Core statement

A targeting choice should be evaluated by its total incremental return, not by the highest response rate within a selected segment. The highest incremental potential often lies with lighter buyers because heavy buyers already purchase frequently and have less room to increase.[^returns][^light-buyers]

## Evaluation procedure

1. Estimate the segment's size, current purchase level, and reachable proportion.
2. Estimate incremental response, not just observed response.
3. Calculate total change across the market, including non-target groups reached incidentally.
4. Compare the gain with the cost and loss of scale from narrow media or distribution.
5. Test whether customer-database selection reflects true incremental potential or simply past purchase frequency.[^returns][^database]

## Conditions and limits

A segment can show a stronger percentage response while contributing fewer total sales because it is small. Media audiences rarely permit perfect separation, so attempts to minimise “wastage” can reduce total reach. Customer databases often overrepresent heavy, loyal buyers and can reinforce a focus on people least able to increase purchasing.[^returns][^database]

## Decision implications

Optimise for total category-buyer reach and incremental sales or usage. Treat historical profitability and response scores as inputs to test, not automatic instructions to concentrate marketing investment.

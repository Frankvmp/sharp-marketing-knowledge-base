# Smart targeting principles

## Core statement

The default target for marketing activity is the whole category market: all people or organisations that buy from the category. Restriction is justified only by compelling, data-driven evidence that it improves overall business opportunity.[^smart]

## Principles

1. Start from inclusion rather than exclusion.
2. Define the market as category buyers, not a narrow demographic description.
3. Offer appropriate variety for heterogeneous needs while keeping individual offers easy to find and buy.
4. Seek scale and efficiency by reaching as many relevant buyers as possible.
5. Restrict targeting only where a real constraint or evidence-supported opportunity warrants it.
6. Treat mass marketing and product variety as compatible, not opposing stages of sophistication.[^smart][^summary]

## Conditions and limits

Narrow targeting can be useful for a distinct offer, specialised requirement, or clearly evidenced opportunity. It becomes a growth risk when it excludes plausible buyers, relies on assumed persona differences, or confuses a tactical media selection with the strategic market target.[^smart]

## Decision implications

Require a clear evidence test before narrowing an audience: what group is excluded, why cannot the offer serve it, what incremental return is expected, and what scale is lost? If those questions cannot be answered, retain broad category reach.

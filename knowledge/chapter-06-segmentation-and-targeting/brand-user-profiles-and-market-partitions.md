# Brand-user profiles and market partitions

## Core statement

Competing brands commonly draw buyers from broadly similar populations, with larger brands reaching more buyers across those populations. Distinct partitions can exist where offers meet materially different needs, but partitions are not evidence that every competing brand owns a unique audience.[^profiles][^partitions]

## Definitions

- **Brand-user profile:** the observed characteristics of a brand's buyers.
- **Market partition:** a division of a category where groups of offers show lower-than-expected overlap because they meet different needs or occasions.
- **Duplication of purchase:** the expected sharing of buyers between brands, used to identify deviations that may indicate a partition.[^profiles][^partitions]

## Reasoning procedure

1. Compare brand buyer profiles against the category population.
2. Check whether apparent differences are primarily brand-size effects.
3. Examine behavioural overlap between offers.
4. Identify genuine deviations that may indicate a need-based partition.
5. Treat broad brand appeal as the default unless data shows a material exception.[^profiles][^partitions]

## Decision implications

Avoid positioning an ordinary brand as relevant only to a narrowly described audience without evidence of a partition. Use real need distinctions to organise variants, but preserve broad access to category buyers where the brands compete as interchangeable alternatives.

# Segmentation-based targeting

## Core statement

Segmentation divides a market into smaller groups using buyer differences that may be relevant to an offering or marketing activity. Its usefulness depends on whether the difference is actionable and valuable, not on the ability to construct a statistical cluster.[^segmentation]

## Analysis procedure

1. Define the category market and the decision to be improved.
2. Identify behavioural, demographic, geographic, organisational, or needs-based differences that could change the offer or access.
3. Test whether the difference is stable enough, large enough, and valuable enough to justify different treatment.
4. Compare the benefit of differentiation with the loss of scale, added cost, and exclusion of other buyers.[^segmentation][^appeal]

## Conditions and limits

Buyer behaviour changes by situation and over time. Clustering can always produce a manageable number of groups, but a cluster does not automatically represent a real or profitable target. Treating two groups as separate can reduce scale and competitiveness when a single inclusive offer would serve both.[^segmentation][^appeal]

## Decision implications

Use segmentation to identify meaningful variation in demand or access. Do not begin with a persona and assume the market must be organised around it; require evidence that a distinct treatment will improve total returns.

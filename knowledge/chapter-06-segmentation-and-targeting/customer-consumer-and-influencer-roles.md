# Customer, consumer, and influencer roles

## Core statement

The person or organisation that purchases an offer can differ from the person who consumes or uses it, and from the people who influence the decision. Target definition must therefore begin with buying roles, not a single assumed end user.[^roles]

## Role mapping

- **Customer:** the party that buys the offer.
- **Consumer or user:** the party that consumes or uses it.
- **Influencer:** a party whose recommendation, preference, authority, or access affects purchase.
- **Decision-maker:** the party with authority to choose, especially in organisational buying.[^roles]

## Conditions and limits

A customer profile can conceal the roles that initiate, pay for, use, recommend, or veto a purchase. In business markets, multiple people and functions can occupy these roles. A product variant may address different usage needs without requiring each variant to target an isolated population.[^roles][^variants]

## Decision implications

Map the complete buying system before setting audience, message, offer, channel, or measurement. Test which role is affected by each marketing action and do not infer customer needs solely from consumer behaviour.

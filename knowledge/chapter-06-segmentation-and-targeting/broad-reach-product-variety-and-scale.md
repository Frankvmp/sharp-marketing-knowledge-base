# Broad reach, product variety, and scale

## Core statement

A broad-market approach can use product variety to meet heterogeneous needs while retaining scale. Product variants do not require narrowly targeted communication or a separate unique customer segment for every offer.[^broad][^variants]

## Reasoning procedure

1. Define the full category market and the different needs or occasions it contains.
2. Use variants where they solve genuine differences in use, preference, or constraints.
3. Assess each variant for its contribution to overall market reach and scale.
4. Design communications and availability to include as many relevant category buyers as possible.
5. Avoid reducing the market to separate brand-owned segments without evidence.[^broad][^variants]

## Conditions and limits

Some offers necessarily serve narrower groups because of age, use, geography, regulation, or specialised requirements. Such limits do not establish that narrow targeting maximises growth. A portfolio can serve the whole market through multiple brands or variants while individual offerings remain broadly accessible within their relevant partition.[^variants]

## Decision implications

Judge a proposed variant or segment strategy by total-market impact, not only its fit with a selected group. Preserve reach and operational efficiency unless a data-supported restriction delivers a greater overall return.

# Social marketing targets and objectives

## Core statement

Effective social marketing begins with a clearly defined behaviour and a target audience, then sets specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-bound goals. Population-wide change can require reaching supporters and influencers beyond the people expected to change directly.[^objectives]

## Definitions

A target behaviour is the observable action to be encouraged, discouraged, or sustained. A target audience is the group whose behaviour is intended to change. SMART goals are specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, and time-bound; SMARTER objectives add evaluation and review.[^objectives]

## Mechanism and relationships

A broad social goal becomes actionable by specifying who will perform which behaviour, how much change is sought, and by when. Knowledge objectives can build understanding and sustain change, while behavioural objectives should describe a realistic action. Family, peers, schools, workplaces, and other stakeholders can create social pressure or practical support, so the campaign audience can be wider than the direct behaviour-change audience.[^objectives]

## Evidence and data

The chapter contrasts population-level campaigns with narrowly targeted interventions and explains that small, practical changes can be easier to achieve and sustain than broad injunctions such as “eat healthier”.[^objectives]

## Conditions and limits

Overly narrow targeting can exclude people who make the target behaviour easier or harder. Broad reach can waste resources unless it is tied to a clear mechanism of support, norm change, or environmental influence.[^objectives]

## Decision implications

An agent should define the behaviour at a measurable level, state the primary and supporting audiences, select realistic objectives, and name the assumptions connecting campaign exposure to behaviour change.

# Social marketing mix

## Core statement

Social marketing uses seven coordinated levers: product, price, place, promotion, people, physical environment, and process. The mix should reduce barriers to voluntary behaviour change and make the desired behaviour easier to understand, remember, and perform.[^mix]

## Definitions

Product is the target behaviour or knowledge. Price includes financial and non-financial costs, such as time, convenience, and sacrifice. Place is where the behaviour or supporting service is accessed. Promotion communicates the behaviour and its benefits. People, physical environment, and process shape support, context, and the steps needed to act.[^mix]

## Mechanism and relationships

Communication can create knowledge and refresh memory, but it may not be enough to change behaviour. The mix therefore combines messages with practical access, supportive people, enabling environments, and simple processes. Messages should link to relevant occasions when the behaviour can occur.[^mix]

## Evidence and data

The chapter describes the seven Ps as a social-marketing adaptation of the marketing mix and shows that campaigns can require collaboration with families, schools, workplaces, industry professionals, government, and changes to the physical environment.[^mix]

## Conditions and limits

A prominent communication campaign can fail if the desired behaviour is costly, inaccessible, socially unsupported, or difficult to perform. Conversely, environmental or process change without clear communication can leave people unaware of the opportunity.[^mix]

## Decision implications

An agent should diagnose barriers across all seven Ps before choosing campaign tactics. It should not default to advertising when access, incentives, supporting people, or the physical environment are the binding constraints.

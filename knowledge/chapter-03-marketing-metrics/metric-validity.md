# Metric validity

## Core statement

A metric's wording, label, or apparent face validity does not establish what it measures. Meaning requires evidence about how the measure varies with other metrics and responds to changes in the marketplace.[^validity]

## Validation procedure

1. State the construct or decision the measure is intended to support.
2. Examine relationships with related and unrelated measures.
3. Test how the measure changes when the relevant market condition changes.
4. Establish benchmarks for the expected level and pattern.
5. Investigate departures as possible measurement, analysis, interpretation, or market effects.[^validity]

## Conditions and limits

Global service or satisfaction questions can move with weather, season, publicity, or advertising rather than direct service delivery. A survey question may elicit an opinion constructed during the survey rather than a stable pre-existing attitude. No proprietary name makes a measure meaningful by itself.[^validity]

## Decision use

Do not act on a metric until its interpretation is known. Use validated measures to generate hypotheses, then test important causal explanations separately.

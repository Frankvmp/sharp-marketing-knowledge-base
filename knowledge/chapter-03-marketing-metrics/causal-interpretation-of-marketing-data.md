# Causal interpretation of marketing data

## Core statement

A correlation or temporal sequence can make a causal explanation plausible, but does not prove it. Marketing decisions require active checks for coincidence, reverse causality, common causes, and insufficient evidence.[^causality]

## Reasoning procedure

1. Confirm that the proposed cause occurs before or with the outcome.
2. Test whether the association could be a coincidence or shared trend.
3. Test whether the outcome could influence the proposed cause.
4. Identify third variables that could cause both.
5. Distinguish a necessary condition from a sufficient condition.
6. Prefer experiments, replications, and individual exposure-and-purchase evidence for important estimates.[^causality][^experiments]

## Conditions and limits

Historical data can support multiple statistical models with conflicting coefficients. A precise return estimate from a single fitted model does not establish a stable causal effect. Predictive performance in comparable future conditions is a necessary test of a model's usefulness, but prediction alone still does not prove mechanism.[^experiments]

## Decision use

Use correlations to generate hypotheses, not to justify a causal conclusion or exact return-on-investment claim. Record uncertainty, then design an experiment or stronger comparison where the decision warrants it.

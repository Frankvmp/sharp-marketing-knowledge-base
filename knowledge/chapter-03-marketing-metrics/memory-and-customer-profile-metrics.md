# Memory and customer-profile metrics

## Core statement

Memory metrics record what buyers think or feel about brands, including associations and attitudes. Customer-profile metrics describe buyers, such as age, income, location, industry, company size, and media use. Both aid marketing decisions but must be interpreted against buyer behaviour and category benchmarks.[^memory][^profile]

## Mechanism

Memory associations can indicate whether a brand is likely to be noticed or thought of in relevant situations. Customer profiles help identify where buyers live, shop, and consume media. Competing brands within the same category often have similar buyer profiles, so the category profile is an important comparison baseline.[^profile]

## Conditions and limits

Attitudes and purchase intentions often add little beyond past behaviour, particularly for predicting behaviour change. Profile differences should not be assumed from stereotypes; they require research and comparison with category buyers.[^memory][^profile]

## Decision use

Use memory measures to diagnose accessible associations and profile measures to diagnose reach or availability gaps. Do not use either as a stand-alone prediction of sales change.

# Brand-size effects and double jeopardy

## Core statement

Brand size is a benchmark for interpreting many behavioural, memory, and attitude metrics. Larger brands generally have more users and score higher on memory and evaluative measures; smaller brands generally have fewer buyers and those buyers buy somewhat less often.[^size][^double-jeopardy]

## Mechanism

Users know more about the brands they use and are more likely to express positive or detailed views. Competing brands share buyers, but larger brands are more widely known, considered, and physically and mentally available. This produces both higher penetration and modestly higher repeat buying for larger brands.[^size][^double-jeopardy]

## Conditions and limits

A raw comparison of attitude or memory scores is misleading when brands have different user bases. Deviations from the benchmark can be interesting, but should first be checked for sampling, measurement, analysis, or interpretation error.[^size]

## Decision use

Adjust or contextualise memory and attitude comparisons for brand penetration and usage. Do not infer poor marketing solely because a small brand has lower perception scores.


# Chapter 3: Marketing metrics

## Scope

This chapter explains how financial, behavioural, memory, profile, activity, and availability measures work together; how to interpret them against benchmarks; and why causal claims require stronger evidence than correlation.[^source]

## Concept map

- [Marketing measurement system](./marketing-measurement-system.md) — A coordinated set of outcome, customer, activity, and availability metrics used to explain present performance and protect future performance.
- [Financial metrics and profit contribution](./financial-metrics-and-profit-contribution.md) — Financial outcomes require careful cost allocation and should be interpreted alongside revenue, market share, and marketing investment.
- [Behavioural metrics](./behavioural-metrics.md) — Metrics based on what buyers actually do, including penetration, purchase frequency, repertoire behaviour, and share of category requirements.
- [Memory and customer-profile metrics](./memory-and-customer-profile-metrics.md) — Memory measures describe accessible brand associations; profile measures describe who category and brand buyers are and how they can be reached.
- [Activity and physical-availability metrics](./activity-and-physical-availability-metrics.md) — Time-series records of marketing actions and purchase accessibility that support diagnosis of market change.
- [Metric validity](./metric-validity.md) — A metric's meaning must be established empirically through its relationship to other measures and market changes, not inferred from its label or wording.
- [Brand-size effects and double jeopardy](./brand-size-effects-and-double-jeopardy.md) — Larger brands generally have more buyers, slightly higher repeat buying, and stronger memory or attitude scores because more people know and use them.
- [Causal interpretation of marketing data](./causal-interpretation-of-marketing-data.md) — A disciplined approach to distinguishing causal effects from correlation, reverse causation, coincidence, and shared causes.

## Coverage rule

Each metric type is separated from the rules used to interpret it. A metric label or correlation is not treated as proof of meaning, prediction, or causality.

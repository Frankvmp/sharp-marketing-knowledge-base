# Financial metrics and profit contribution

## Core statement

Financial metrics include sales revenue, profit, and cash-flow impact. Profit is straightforward for a discrete business but can be ambiguous for a brand, product, or business unit because shared costs must be allocated.[^financial]

## Definitions

- **Profit** equals sales revenue minus costs.
- **Profit contribution** allocates only direct costs associated with producing and marketing an activity, product, or brand.
- **Overheads** are shared costs whose allocation can change reported profitability.[^financial]

## Reasoning rule

Interpret profit with revenue, market share, and marketing expenditure. A profitable result may reflect efficiency, but it can also reflect reduced marketing investment that will later erode assets and sales. Financial measures should inform marketing decisions without replacing customer and market evidence.[^system]

## Conditions and limits

A cost-allocation method can make a unit appear more or less profitable without changing the underlying economics. Converting suitable marketing measures into financial values is useful, but speculative accounting assumptions or opaque models do not establish a causal return on marketing.[^financial][^behavioural]

# Behavioural metrics

## Core statement

Behavioural metrics measure what buyers actually do: who buys, how often, how much, at what price, from which retailer, and alongside which alternatives. They provide a direct basis for understanding brand performance and buyer behaviour.[^behavioural]

## Evidence sources

Panel data, transaction records, and comparable observed purchase data can show purchase timing, brand choice, quantity, retailer choice, and repertoire structure. They are more informative about behaviour than perception measures, but still require definitions of period, population, and category.[^behavioural]

## Key interpretation rule

A loyalty metric such as share of category requirements must state its time period. Over longer periods, buyers have more purchase occasions and usually reveal larger repertoires, so the average share assigned to any one brand declines. Measures therefore cannot be compared meaningfully when their periods differ.[^scr]

## Conditions and limits

Behavioural metrics answer what buyers did, not necessarily why they did it or what they will do under changed conditions. Search activity and online discussion can indicate attention or interest but may be ambiguous without a behavioural outcome and a benchmark.[^behavioural]

## Decision use

Use behavioural measures to establish the buyer base and performance pattern before interpreting attitudes, intentions, or brand-health claims.

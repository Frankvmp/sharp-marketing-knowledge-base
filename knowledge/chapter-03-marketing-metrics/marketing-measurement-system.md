# Marketing measurement system

## Core statement

A marketing measurement system combines financial outcomes, buyer behaviour, memory, customer profiles, marketing activity, and physical availability. No single measure is sufficient because short-term outcomes can conceal deterioration in the assets that support future sales.[^system]

## Mechanism

Marketing and competitor actions affect buyer behaviour and financial outcomes. Existing market-based assets moderate those effects. Revenue, market share, activity, spend, and asset measures therefore provide context for one another: reduced spend can improve current profit while weakening future sales, share, and availability.[^system]

## Measurement procedure

1. Record what the firm and competitors do.
2. Record buyer behaviour and financial outcomes.
3. Record the state of market-based assets.
4. Interpret change across the combined measures and over time.
5. Investigate whether an apparent efficiency gain is maintained performance or deferred erosion.[^system]

## Conditions and limits

The system focuses on marketing-relevant measures, not every operational metric in an organisation. A reliable measure is not automatically meaningful; its interpretation still requires benchmarks and knowledge of what it reflects.[^system]

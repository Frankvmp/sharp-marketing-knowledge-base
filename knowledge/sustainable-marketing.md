# Sustainable marketing

## Core statement

Sustainable marketing aims to provide long-term customer satisfaction. It requires a financially sustainable business model, sustainable customer demand, and consideration of production, marketing, and consumption effects on society and the environment.[^sharp-2017-ch1-sustainable]

The chapter rejects the idea that marketing always means giving customers whatever they want immediately. Present desires can harm customers or the planet over time, and a business that gives away value without a sustainable model cannot continue to satisfy customers.[^sharp-2017-ch1-sustainable]

## Reasoning rule

An agent should evaluate a proposed marketing action across four horizons:

| Test | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Customer satisfaction | Does it create or protect value for customers over time? |
| Business model | Can the organisation sustain the economics of the action? |
| Demand | Is the pattern of demand itself sustainable, rather than only immediately attractive? |
| Wider effects | What effects could production, marketing, and consumption have on society and the environment? |

## Evidence and data

The chapter uses lending, vehicle safety, and product side effects to show that current demand and immediate profit can create longer-term harm. The vehicle example connects safety improvement to product development, consumer communication, public education, and support for changes to the wider usage environment.[^sharp-2017-ch1-sustainable]

## Decision boundary

A proposal that raises short-term sales or satisfaction is not, by that fact alone, sustainable. The agent should record unresolved long-term risks rather than treating them as automatically acceptable trade-offs.[^sharp-2017-ch1-sustainable]

## Scope and limits

This is Sharp's Chapter 1 framing. It is not a complete ethical theory, environmental-impact assessment, or legal compliance procedure.[^sharp-2017-ch1-sustainable]

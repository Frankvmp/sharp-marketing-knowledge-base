# Buyer behaviour evidence

## Core statement

Buyer behaviour should be understood from descriptive evidence about current and potential buyers, interpreted against known recurring patterns. Internal opinion is not a substitute for this evidence because people working on a brand have an unusually involved relationship with it.[^buyer-evidence]

## Evidence requirements

Useful descriptive evidence includes who buys, where they buy, when they buy, how much they buy, what they buy, what they watch, and how the category fits into their lives. Surveys can record behaviour, perceptions, and opinions. The data becomes decision-relevant only when it is interpreted through established patterns that indicate what is ordinary, unusual, or consequential.[^buyer-evidence]

## Reasoning procedure

1. Specify the buyer behaviour or decision that needs explanation.
2. Gather descriptive data on actual and potential buyers.
3. Compare the observation with established buying patterns relevant to the category, store choice, or industrial purchase.
4. Separate observed behaviour from internal explanations of why it happened.
5. Treat intuition, executive opinion, and colleague views as hypotheses to test, not evidence.

## Conditions and limits

A marketer does not need to reconstruct the psychology of every individual buyer. The practical objective is a progressively better understanding of how factors affect buying behaviour across a market. Patterns guide interpretation; they do not remove the need to examine the specific category and situation.[^buyer-evidence]

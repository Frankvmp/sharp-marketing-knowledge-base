# Shopping laws and navigation

## Core statement

Shoppers seek products within constrained time and cognitive effort. Failure to find a wanted item, out-of-stock conditions, changed locations, and poor navigation reduce repeat business.[^shopping][^navigation]

## Operational implications

Keep core items available, category locations stable, and navigation cues clear. Most trips involve seeking a few items from a very large assortment, so colours, symbols, shapes, distinctive assets, shelf layout, and store wayfinding reduce search effort.[^shopping][^navigation]

## Assortment concentration and store flow

A small group of top-selling items contributes a disproportionate share of retail sales. Make those items easy to see and find; hiding them to force a search can reduce their sales and frustrate shoppers.[^top-items]

Shoppers tend to follow open paths and move towards the checkout. Product placement should account for those flows and make products visible where shoppers naturally travel rather than assuming shoppers will alter their route.[^paths]

## Conditions and limits

Shopping behaviour includes browsing and cross-purchasing, but a retailer should not interpret shopper time as freely expandable. Specials can change behaviour, yet do not remove the need for reliable assortment and ease of finding products.[^shopping]

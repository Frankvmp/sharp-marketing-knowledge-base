# Retail competition and store choice

## Core statement

Store choice shows regular repeat-purchase and repertoire patterns. Smaller retailers tend to have fewer buyers and somewhat lower loyalty, while stores share more buyers with larger and physically closer competitors.[^competition]

## Evidence and interpretation

Double jeopardy and duplication of purchase apply to retailers as well as brands. Retail chains compete through mental and physical availability; functional differences still matter, but availability explains much of reach and repeat behaviour. Location and outlet numbers influence shopper penetration, loyalty, and competitive overlap.[^competition]

## Decision implications

Benchmark retailer performance using penetration, frequency, loyalty, location coverage, and buyer sharing. Avoid explaining a smaller store's performance solely through a uniquely narrow customer segment without checking availability and category conditions.

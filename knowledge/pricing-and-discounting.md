# Pricing and discounting

## Core statement

Pricing must cover overall costs and seek a surplus, while accounting for the competitive landscape, buyer value, and the effect of price on sales volume. Cost-based formulas can be useful, but they do not establish the price buyers will pay or the price that maximises profit.[^pricing]

## Reasoning procedure

1. Establish relevant costs and recognise that cost allocation and volume affect the result.
2. Assess the offer's value and risks for buyers relative to competing options.
3. Estimate price elasticity: the sales response to a price change.
4. Identify reference prices and price-leader thresholds that can create large sales changes.
5. Assess price promotion separately from enduring demand and profitability.[^pricing-summary]

## Conditions and limits

A lower purchase price can have a higher total cost of ownership where quality, maintenance, productive life, or supply continuity differ. Price promotions often have temporary effects, may fail to add new buyers, and can reduce profits substantially. Pricing requires judgement because cost, volume, competition, and buyer response interact.[^pricing][^pricing-summary]

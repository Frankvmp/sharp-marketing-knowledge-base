# International market expansion

## Core statement

Companies enter foreign markets to seek growth beyond a constrained home market, access a larger or differently structured customer base, and gain production efficiencies. International expansion also increases decision complexity because market conditions differ across countries.[^expansion]

## Definitions

Global marketing concerns marketing across national markets. A domestic market is the home-country context from which expansion begins. A foreign market is a country or region in which the company must operate under different market, institutional, and cultural conditions.[^expansion]

## Mechanism and relationships

Limited home-market demand can constrain growth, especially for firms serving small countries, narrow categories, or saturated markets. Foreign markets can provide more buyers, different needs, and higher production volume. These potential benefits must be evaluated alongside the additional uncertainty and adaptation required in each market.[^expansion]

## Evidence and data

World trade grew much faster than world output between 1950 and 2006, illustrating the increasing relevance of cross-border trade. The chapter identifies growth and scale efficiencies as two principal motives for international expansion.[^expansion]

## Conditions and limits

Market size alone does not make expansion attractive. A larger foreign market may differ in competition, information quality, regulation, infrastructure, economic conditions, and buyer expectations.[^expansion]

## Decision implications

An agent should assess foreign-market opportunity and operating conditions together. It should not recommend expansion solely from market size or expected scale economies.
